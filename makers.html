Home/Makers

Seven workshops

Most furniture arrives anonymous. The premise of this shop is that it should not: every piece names the workshop that built it, where that workshop is, and how long it has been going.

Where they are

Four provinces, from a cane workshop in the Cederberg to a steel shop in Mbombela. Pick a town to see what comes out of it.

Why the name goes on the piece

A retailer that hides its suppliers can swap them out quietly, and usually does — the price stays the same and the joint gets cheaper. Naming the workshop makes that swap visible. It also means a fault has an address: if a frame fails in year eight, you know whose bench to phone rather than which call centre to queue for.

It is a harder promise to keep than it looks. It means fewer suppliers, longer lead times on popular pieces, and no quiet substitution when a run sells out. That trade is the whole idea.

These workshops are invented. Thorn & Grain is a concept built to demonstrate design and build work, and the seven workshops named on this site do not exist. The towns are real; the businesses are not. No real workshop has been described, quoted or represented here.
